Sales Performance Dashboard 2024

Context: Microsoft Excel Bootcamp, DSAREA (2026), grade A  |  Tools: Microsoft Excel

  • 400transactions in the dataset
  • 256completed transactions analyzed
  • 3reference tables linked

Project Overview

A Microsoft Excel project built during the DSAREA Excel bootcamp. I worked with a dataset of 400 electronics retail transactions and focused the analysis on the 256 transactions with the status Selesai (completed) between January and December 2024. The goal was to turn a flat transaction list into a formula-driven workbook and an executive dashboard.

The Challenge

The raw data mixed completed, in-process, cancelled, and returned transactions (Selesai = completed, Proses = in process, Batal = cancelled, and Return = returned), while product, sales, and region details lived in separate reference tables. The dashboard had to report only completed transactions and keep every figure traceable to the source data.

The Action

  • Relational lookups: built a formula-driven transaction table linked to three reference tables (products, sales, and regions) with VLOOKUP and INDEX-MATCH.
  • Calculations: computed total sales, cost of goods sold (COGS), profit, and margin for each transaction.
  • Executive dashboard: used SUMIFS and COUNTIF so the KPIs count only completed transactions.
  • Error handling: wrapped the lookups in IFERROR so the dashboard stays free of error values.
  • Exploration: added Pivot Tables and slicers for interactive filtering.

The Impact & Results

The dashboard summarizes the completed transactions in one view:

  • Rp8.76 billion total sales
  • Rp2.82 billion total profit
  • 256 completed transactions
  • 32.2% average margin

Global Human Development Dashboard

Context: Final project, Data Warehouse & Business Intelligence course (individual), July 2026  |  Tools: Tableau

  • 167countries
  • 10variables
  • 10worksheets
  • 2interactive dashboards

Open the live dashboard on Tableau Public

Project Overview

The final project for the Data Warehouse and Business Intelligence course, completed individually in July 2026. I analyzed a socio-economic dataset of 167 countries and 10 variables from Kaggle, and built an interactive Tableau dashboard to compare economic prosperity and quality of life across countries.

The Challenge

GDP per capita can differ by hundreds of times between countries, which makes patterns hard to see in a table. The project set out to show, in a few clear views, how a country’s wealth relates to the well-being of its people.

The Action

  • Structured flow: followed business understanding, data understanding, data preparation, analysis, findings, and recommendations.
  • Data quality check: confirmed there were no missing values or duplicate rows across the 167 countries.
  • Preparation in Tableau: set the Geographic Role for country names and created a calculated field for trade balance (exports minus imports, as a percentage of GDP).
  • Outlier decision: Singapore and Luxembourg show export and import values above 100% of GDP. After checking, I kept them because they reflect trading-hub economies, not input errors.
  • Visualization: built 10 worksheets and 2 interactive dashboards (Global Economic Overview, and Health & Social Indicators) using a map, Top N rankings, scatter plots, and average reference lines.
  • Business questions: answered 8 questions defined at the start, such as which countries are most prosperous and most economically vulnerable.

The Impact & Results

The analysis produced 5 findings and 5 recommendations for stakeholders such as international organizations, governments, researchers, and investors. Some of the findings:

  • Higher GDP per capita goes together with longer life expectancy, and the few low-GDP countries with long life expectancy are worth studying.
  • Spending more on health does not automatically give the highest life expectancy.
  • Wealthier countries tend to have fertility rates below the replacement level of 2.1.
  • Extreme inflation is concentrated in lower-income developing countries.

Data Management & Cleansing: HIMSI Esports Tournament

Role: Public Relations Staff, HEC UIN Jakarta (Aug – Oct 2025)  |  Tools: Microsoft Excel

  • 36participants
  • 3game categories
  • Rp1,000,000payments reconciled

Project Overview

I prepared the registration data for a campus esports tournament organized by HIMSI at UIN Jakarta, featuring FIFA, Mobile Legends, and Valorant. As Public Relations Staff, I cleaned and validated the data of 36 participants so the match division could build the tournament brackets.

The Challenge

The raw registration data collected through Google Forms was unstructured. It mixed solo players with team registrations, used inconsistent text formats (random capitalization and extra spaces), and had overlapping columns. The database had to be restructured quickly and accurately so the committee could continue without administrative delays.

The Action

  • Data cleansing: normalized the text entries with the PROPER and TRIM formulas to fix capitalization and remove extra spaces.
  • Data transformation: separated the master data into three structured tables, one for each game.
  • Payment validation: validated and reconciled participant payments made through QRIS and DANA.
  • Privacy: masked personally identifiable information (PII) in the shared views.
  • Reporting: built Pivot Tables and a simple visual dashboard with Pie and Bar charts.

Note: personally identifiable information such as phone numbers, emails, and student IDs has been masked or anonymized in these images to protect participant privacy.

The Impact & Results

  • Gave the match division a clean, ready-to-use database to build the brackets.
  • Reconciled the registration payments, totaling Rp1,000,000 across QRIS and DANA, with 100% accuracy and no discrepancies.
  • Produced visual summaries of participant demographics and game popularity, used as input for the committee’s evaluation.

HR Business Process Analysis & BPMN Modeling

Role: HR Analyst, Enterprise Systems Course Project (2026)  |  Tools: Search Engines, draw.io (BPMN 2.0)

  • 6integrated modules
  • 2BPMN 2.0 diagrams
  • 4members in the HR team

Project Overview

An Enterprise Systems course project focused on analyzing and designing an integrated Human Resource (HR) module a bottled-water manufacturing company. The project was part of a six-module enterprise system initiative involving HR, Purchasing, Finance, Production, Inventory, and Sales & Marketing.

As the HR Analyst and the only analyst in a four-member HR team, I conducted preliminary desk research using search engines and publicly available references to understand the company's organizational structure, HR functions, and operational activities. The research provided a foundation for analyzing business processes and developing the proposed HR workflows.

The Challenge

The HR function covered several interconnected activities, including recruitment, employee management, attendance and payroll, performance evaluation, shift scheduling, occupational health and safety (K3L), and legal administration. These processes also involved coordination with Finance and Production.

The main challenge was to understand the existing workflows, identify operational problems and integration gaps, and translate the findings into structured process models. The proposed workflows also needed to show how the HR module could connect with other enterprise modules.

The Action

  • Preliminary Research: explored publicly available references through search engines to understand the company's organizational structure, HR responsibilities, and business operations.
  • Business Process Analysis: examined key HR activities and identified issues such as manual data entry, disconnected employee records, payroll coordination gaps, and separate shift scheduling between HR and Production.
  • AS-IS Process Modeling: developed a BPMN 2.0 diagram in draw.io to illustrate existing workflows, covering recruitment, attendance and payroll, KPI evaluation, shift scheduling, K3L, and legal administration.
  • TO-BE Process Design: proposed improved HR workflows through an integrated enterprise system, including digital recruitment, automated attendance and payroll synchronization, KPI evaluation, shift scheduling, and integrated K3L and legal processes.
  • Cross-Module Integration: mapped the proposed interactions between HR, Finance, and Production to support connected data flows and better coordination across departments.
  • Team Handover: prepared and presented the process diagrams and analysis documentation as references for the Design and Programmer teams, who continued the project through the subsequent system design and development stages.

The Impact & Results

  • Delivered two BPMN 2.0 diagrams representing the existing (AS-IS) and proposed (TO-BE) HR business processes.
  • Produced a structured analysis of HR workflows and identified key integration opportunities with Finance and Production.
  • Provided process documentation that served as a reference for the Design and Programmer teams in the next stages of the project.

My responsibility focused on preliminary research, business process analysis, and BPMN 2.0 modeling. The subsequent system design and programming stages were handled by the respective team members. This was an academic system analysis and design project, not a real ERP implementation.
